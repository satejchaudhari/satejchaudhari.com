Red Team

Red Team Methodology & Threat Emulation

2026-10-09 · 10 min read

A red-team engagement is not a pentest with cooler tools. A pentest finds as many vulnerabilities as possible in a scope; a red team pursues a specific objective the way a real adversary would — quietly, end-to-end — to test whether the organisation can detect and respond, not just whether a bug exists. This page is the shape of that engagement and how it ties together the rest of the red-team material here.

Red team vs. pentest vs. purple team

ModeGoal
Penetration testBreadth — find and prove as many vulnerabilities as possible in scope.
Red teamObjective-driven — reach a defined goal (the "crown jewels") emulating a real threat, testing people, process and tech including detection.
Purple teamCollaborative — red and blue work together to build and validate detections technique by technique (see Detection Engineering).

The engagement lifecycle

  1. Scoping & rules of engagement — objective (the "flag"), in-scope targets and techniques, deconfliction, and a get-out-of-jail contact. Decide the start posture: external, assumed-breach, or a specific scenario.
  2. Threat modelling & emulation plan — pick a relevant adversary and map the TTPs you will emulate to MITRE ATT&CK, so the test mirrors a plausible real attacker.
  3. Reconnaissance — OSINT and attack-surface mapping (the recon half of the workflow).
  4. Initial access — the vector that fits the surface (the initial-access half of the workflow).
  5. Foothold, privesc & persistence — stabilise, escalate (Linux / Windows), and persist appropriately for the scenario.
  6. Enumerate & move — graph the environment (BloodHound), harvest credentials (LSASS/DPAPI), pivot, and move toward the objective (the AD map).
  7. Actions on objective — reach and demonstrate access to the crown jewels; collect/exfiltrate as proof.
  8. Reporting & retest — narrative of the path, the detections that did and did not fire, remediation, and a replay to confirm fixes.

ATT&CK as the common language

Mapping every action to MITRE ATT&CK gives red and blue a shared vocabulary: the red team can emulate a named actor's technique set, and the blue team can measure coverage technique-by-technique. Adversary-emulation plans (e.g. the CTID library) turn "act like this threat group" into a concrete, ordered list of TTPs to execute and detect.

The deliverable that matters is not "we got Domain Admin" — it is a timeline of what you did, what the defenders saw, and where the gaps were. A red team that reports only the kill chain, and not the detection outcome at each step, has done half the job.

OPSEC & safety throughout

  • Emulate the threat, but stay within RoE — no destructive actions, careful with production, deconflict with the blue team's live alerts.
  • Keep meticulous logs for deconfliction and the report; every action timestamped and attributable to the engagement.
  • Balance stealth against progress to the objective — the point is realism, which includes getting caught sometimes and seeing what happens.
